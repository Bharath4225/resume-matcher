    NLP Pipeline Interview Prep Guide

    This guide breaks down every step of the NLP pipeline so you can comfortably explain your design choices in technical interviews (e.g. TCS Prime, product tech rounds).

    1. Pipeline Architecture Steps

    Step 1
    Text Preprocessing & Tokenization: Converts text to lower case, preserves domain tokens (like C++, Node.js), strips stop-words, and removes formatting noise.
    Step 2
    TF-IDF Vectorization:
    \(\text{TF}(t, d) = \frac{\text{count}(t, d)}{\text{total words in } d}\)  |  \(\text{IDF}(t) = \log\left(\frac{N}{\text{df}(t)}\right) + 1\)
    Sublinear TF scaling (\(1 + \log(\text{tf})\)) prevents high-frequency terms from overwhelming score vectors.
    Step 3
    Cosine Similarity Math:
    \(\text{Cosine Similarity}(\vec{A}, \vec{B}) = \frac{\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B}}{\|\vec{A}\| \|\vec{B}\|} = \frac{\sum A_i B_i}{\sqrt{\sum A_i^2} \sqrt{\sum B_i^2}}\)
    Evaluates the angle between document vectors in high-dimensional feature space, making it length-invariant.

    2. Key Interview Questions & Answers

    Q: Why did you choose TF-IDF + Cosine Similarity over BERT or Transformer embeddings?
    Answer: TF-IDF provides 100% deterministic interpretability. In ATS applications, candidates and recruiters need to see exactly which terms contributed to the match score. Transformers can introduce semantic hallucination (e.g. treating 'React' and 'Angular' as identical because both are frontend frameworks), whereas TF-IDF cleanly flags exact missing technical keywords. Furthermore, TF-IDF runs in sub-milliseconds without requiring expensive GPU compute.
    Q: How do you handle words missing from the dictionary or different word forms?
    Answer: We combine TF-IDF with a rule-based and spaCy technical taxonomy extractor. Lemmatization and token normalization map inflected forms back to canonical roots, while n-gram range (1, 2) captures multi-word phrases like 'Machine Learning' or 'Spring Boot'.
